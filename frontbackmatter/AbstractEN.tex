\begin{otherlanguage}{american}
    \chapter*{Abstract}

    Abstract argumentation frameworks (AFs) model conflicts through
    arguments and directed attacks. Conditional independence asks whether
    assessments of disjoint argument sets \(X\) and \(Y\), taken from valid
    labelings that agree on a conditioning set \(Z\), can be recombined in
    another valid labeling. Because graph-based criteria capture only some
    cases in which independence holds, an exact decision procedure is
    required.

    This thesis develops a uniform encoding using quantified Boolean
    formulas (QBFs) for the \emph{complete}, \emph{stable}, and
    \emph{preferred} semantics. Boolean variables encode the labels
    \(\mathsf{in}\), \(\mathsf{out}\), and \(\mathsf{und}\); semantic,
    equality, and mixing constraints connect two universally quantified
    source labelings with an existentially quantified recombination
    labeling. For the \emph{preferred} semantics, the inclusion-maximality
    of the set of accepted arguments is additionally encoded. A correctness
    proof shows that the resulting QBF is true exactly when the requested
    conditional independence holds, without requiring all valid labelings
    to be enumerated explicitly.

    The encodings are generated in the \texttt{QCIR-G14} format and
    evaluated with QuAbS. The empirical evaluation uses \(105\) AFs from
    the benchmark collection of the International Competition on
    Computational Models of Argumentation 2025 (ICCMA 2025). For each AF
    under each of the three semantics, five reproducibly sampled unordered
    argument pairs are combined with six independently sampled, non-nested
    conditioning sets. This yields \(9\,450\) queries, of which \(7\,164\)
    are decided: \(6\,986\) (\(97.52\,\%\)) as independent and \(178\)
    (\(2.48\,\%\)) as dependent. Among the decided queries, larger
    conditioning sets are associated with a lower proportion of dependent
    outcomes; they are also associated with longer solver runtimes and lower
    cumulative decision rates. Because the conditioning sets are non-nested and the
    logical status of undecided queries is unknown, these associations are
    descriptive rather than causal.

    The complexity analysis shows that every independence query has a
    positive answer for acyclic and, more generally, even-cycle-free AFs,
    whereas the problem restricted to odd-cycle-free AFs is
    \(\Pi_2^{\mathrm{P}}\)-complete under all three semantics.
\end{otherlanguage}
