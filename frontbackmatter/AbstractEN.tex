\begin{otherlanguage}{american}
    \chapter*{Abstract}

    Abstract argumentation frameworks model reasoning with conflicting
    information through arguments and directed attacks. A semantics
    determines which overall assessments of the arguments are valid;
    labelings represent these assessments by marking each argument as
    accepted, rejected, or undecided. Two argument sets are conditionally
    independent if their respective assessments from any two valid
    labelings that agree on a third set can be combined in another valid
    labeling. The assessment of this third set provides the context and
    must remain unchanged.

    The theoretical properties of conditional independence in these
    frameworks remain only partly explored. Existing graph-based criteria
    also capture only some cases in which independence holds. This thesis
    investigates the computational complexity of the problem and develops
    an exact decision procedure.

    For the widely studied \emph{complete}, \emph{stable}, and
    \emph{preferred} semantics, independence queries are uniformly
    translated into quantified Boolean formulas (QBFs) and evaluated using
    the QBF solver QuAbS. A correctness proof establishes that the resulting
    formula is true if and only if the queried independence holds.

    The complexity analysis shows that independence holds for every query
    in frameworks without directed cycles of even length. In frameworks
    without directed cycles of odd length, however, deciding independence
    remains computationally hard (\(\Pi_2^{\mathrm{P}}\)-complete) under all
    three semantics.

    An empirical evaluation of \(9\,450\) queries on \(105\) frameworks
    resolves approximately three quarters of the queries. Of these, about
    \(98\,\%\) confirm conditional independence. Larger context sets are
    associated with less observed dependence among the resolved queries.
    They also tend to coincide with longer runtimes and a smaller proportion
    of resolved queries.
\end{otherlanguage}
